About this atlas and its statistics +

The literature timelines on the main page preserve the paper’s frozen HFpEF and sepsis figures. Their clinically adjusted odds ratios are estimated for reconstructed literature markers and Glyph concepts in the study cohort; they are not the effect estimates originally published in the cited studies. References link to the source papers.

The atlas preserves the original seed-1283 paper layout: learned concepts and clinical context variables. Coordinates and connections stay fixed as colors, outcomes and display filters change. Dashed concept outlines retain the original layout's undocumented-concept designation.

Association estimates are from the Discovery cohort and describe outcomes occurring 1–4 years after CT. Odds ratios are per standard deviation of concept activation. Disease hues identify outcome families, while darker shades indicate higher odds. Shading is scaled separately for each outcome, from white at OR 1 to the strongest significant effect. The atlas displays higher-risk estimates (OR > 1). Disease coloring uses Bonferroni-significant associations; the correction covers all 604,185 tested concept–outcome pairs. Inspect a concept's profile to see nonsignificant estimates too. These are associations, not causal effects.

The larger study in manuscript v34 reports 397 high-risk concepts and 4,463 Discovery associations; this atlas retains the original saved paper layout. Curated revised interpretations are used where available. Original AutoInterp accuracies, where shown, measure report-based discrimination. They do not validate revised wording or independently establish an imaging biomarker. The five clinical variables have newly computed unadjusted associations with all 141 outcomes, using the same isolated logistic estimator and Discovery population as the concept analysis. Clinical estimates are per standard deviation and use a Bonferroni denominator of 604,890 (the 604,185 original tests plus 705 clinical tests); frozen concept estimates retain their original correction.
